How this works

Where the numbers come from. Delhi's bus e-ticketing data for one full month — October 2026. That is 25.6 lakh tickets (2,559,899 in all), each with a route and a time. We counted how many people boarded every route in every hour of the day.

How a band is decided. Each route is judged against its own day. Its 8 busiest hours are marked "bahut bheed", the next 8 "khadi bheed", and its 8 quietest hours "aaram se seat milegi". So the band answers the question a daily traveller actually asks: for this route, is this a busy hour or a quiet one? A small route at its own busiest hour still shows "bahut bheed" — because for that route, it is.

What to keep in mind. E-tickets are only about 3–4% of all tickets sold, so this is a sample, not a full count of every passenger. The bands show the usual pattern from last October. They cannot see today's crowd, a cancelled bus, rain, or a festival day. Treat this as a good guess from past data — not a live promise.

What this is. A working prototype for the Delhi Transport Stack Hackathon, Problem 2 — the crowdedness-aware journey planner. Built by Team ABHI X AAYUSHI.
